## 1. Introduction

A pot is a small world.

The soil inside is limited, drainage is fast, and the plant depends entirely on your hands. Not on rain, not on ambient humidity — on you.

And the key to watering potted plants is not frequency — it's judgment.

Whoever learns to read the soil and feel the weight of the pot will turn their hands into the best watering tool there is.

## 3. Clear Explanation

In the ground, the soil is a large reservoir — roots can go deep, moisture disperses to wide areas, and there is always a reserve.

In a pot, none of this exists. The soil is limited, and what isn't absorbed drains out. Therefore, the goal is not to maintain constant moisture — but to create a cycle of deep watering, then partial drying. This is how healthy roots develop and root rot from overwatering is prevented.

## 4. Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1 — Check Before You Water
- **Action:** Insert a finger 2–4 cm into the soil
- **What to look for:** If the layer is dry — water. If still moist — wait
- **Additional sign:** Lift the pot. A light pot is a thirsty pot
- **Important to remember:** Small pots dry out fast. Large pots hold moisture longer

### Step 2 — Water Deep and Slow
- **Action:** Water slowly across the entire soil surface
- **How much:** Continue until water exits from the drainage holes
- **Pro tip:** Wait a minute or two then add a little more — when the soil was very dry, saturation comes in waves
- **Purpose:** To ensure the entire root zone received water, not just the top layer

### Step 3 — Empty the Saucer
- **Action:** About twenty minutes after watering, make sure there is no standing water in the saucer
- **Why:** Roots sitting in water without oxygen rot
- **The fine line:** A saucer is fine. Standing water for hours — that's a problem

### Step 4 — Wait for Partial Drying
- **Action:** Do not water again until the top layer starts to dry out
- **What to look for:** Soil that starts to pull away from the pot walls, lighter color on the surface
- **Principle:** Partial drying — yes. Complete drying — no

## 5. Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1 — A Little Water Every Day
- **What people do:** "Spread" water over the surface every morning
- **Why it fails:** The top layer gets moisture, but the roots in the depths are dry
- **The right way:** Deep watering, less frequent

### Mistake 2 — Water on a Fixed Schedule
- **What people do:** Water every two days regardless of soil condition
- **Why it fails:** Different pots dry at different rates — container material, sun position, plant type all matter
- **The right way:** Check the soil, not the calendar

### Mistake 3 — Leave Water in the Saucer
- **What people do:** Forget to empty the saucer
- **Why it fails:** Roots soaked without oxygen — rot, yellowing, decline
- **The right way:** Always empty. If the saucer fills again after an hour — still okay, after a night — empty it

### Mistake 4 — Use Garden Soil in a Pot
- **What people do:** Fill a pot with regular soil from the garden
- **Why it fails:** Garden soil compacts in a container, doesn't allow drainage, and suffocates roots
- **The right way:** Ready-made potting mix — holds moisture but also drains

### Mistake 5 — Ignore the Container Type
- **What people do:** Water according to the plant without considering the pot itself
- **Why it fails:** Terracotta dries twice as fast as plastic. Fabric pots — even faster
- **The right way:** Adjust check frequency to container material

## 6. Biodynamic Insight

In the biodynamic garden, watering is not just maintenance — it is touch.

When you touch the soil, feel the moisture, lift the pot and sense its weight — you are in dialogue with the plant. It speaks to you through the soil, and you respond.

The best watering time for most plants is early morning. The soil is receptive, the heat hasn't peaked yet, and the plant can breathe before the burden of the day.

On especially hot days — check again at midday to early afternoon. Not necessarily to water — but to know.

## 7. Result / Change

When you water correctly, the plant speaks.

The leaves are more upright and in a deeper shade.
Growth is steady, not erratic.
The soil smells good — the smell of earth after rain, not the smell of rot.

And with time — the hands learn. The lift of a moist pot and the lift of a dry pot become a language.
This is one of the most useful skills a home gardener can acquire.

## 8. Quick Summary

- **What to do:** Check before you water — finger in the soil, feel the weight of the pot
- **How to water:** Deep and slow, until water exits from drainage
- **When:** When the top layer starts to dry out — not by the clock
- **Why it works:** Deep and healthy roots, prevents rot, stronger plant
