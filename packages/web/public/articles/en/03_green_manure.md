## 3. Clear Explanation

Green manure is the practice of growing plants specifically to return them to the soil instead of harvesting them.

These plants can:
- protect the soil surface
- add organic matter
- feed microbial life
- improve structure
- help with nitrogen capture when legumes are used

It is a way to grow part of your fertility inside the garden itself.

## 4. Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1 — Choose the right cover crop
- **Action:** Select plants suited to your season and goal
- **Details:** Clover, vetch, peas, and fava beans are common options.
- **Signals:** Fast, even growth across the bed

### Step 2 — Prepare and sow
- **Action:** Lightly prepare the soil and scatter seeds evenly
- **Details:** Good seed-to-soil contact matters more than deep tillage.
- **Signals:** Uniform germination

### Step 3 — Let the crop grow
- **Action:** Allow the plants to build biomass
- **Details:** Early flowering is often the ideal moment.
- **Signals:** Dense green coverage and first flowers

### Step 4 — Cut at the right time
- **Action:** Cut the plants down
- **Details:** Do not wait until they become woody and dry.
- **Signals:** Soft stems, still-green growth

### Step 5 — Return it to the soil
- **Action:** Leave as mulch or lightly incorporate
- **Details:** Work in balanced moisture, not dust-dry or muddy soil.
- **Signals:** Material begins to soften into the bed

## 5. Mistakes to Avoid

- **Waiting too long to cut:** Old fibrous plants break down slowly.
- **Choosing the wrong species:** Weak establishment gives weak benefit.
- **Working poor soil moisture:** Structure can suffer.
- **Expecting instant repair:** Soil building is cumulative.

## 6. Biodynamic Insight

Green manure aligns beautifully with biodynamic thinking because it puts soil life at the center. The timing of cutting matters: you want the plant while it is still full of vitality, not after it has become tired and dry.

Not everything valuable is harvested above ground.

## 7. Results / Transformation

The first change is often in the feel of the soil.

It becomes less harsh.  
Less exposed.  
More crumbly and alive.

Then come better water holding and steadier growth in the next crop.

## 8. Quick Summary

- **What to do:** Grow a cover crop and return it to the soil before the main crop
- **When to do it:** Between planting cycles or in periods dedicated to soil-building
- **Why it works:** It adds biomass, protects the surface, feeds microbes, and improves structure
