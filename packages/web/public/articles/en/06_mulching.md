# Mulching — Protect and Feed Your Soil

**SEO Title:** Mulching Guide for Healthy Soil  
**Meta Description:** Learn how mulching protects soil, improves fertility, and reduces water loss naturally.

## 1. Hook
In nature, soil is never bare. A soft layer of fallen leaves and organic matter protects everything below.
Mulching is simply copying that quiet intelligence—shielding the soil while feeding the life within it.

## 2. Visual Guide
- Hero: straw or wood-chip mulch covering a garden bed

## 3. Clear Explanation
Mulching is covering soil with organic material such as straw, leaves, compost, or wood chips.
It reduces evaporation, stabilizes temperature, suppresses weeds, and feeds microbes as it breaks down.
Instead of fighting climate, mulch buffers it—turning exposed soil into a protected micro-ecosystem.

## 4. Step-by-Step Guide
1) Prepare soil: remove weeds, water lightly so soil is moist  
2) Choose material: straw (vegetables), leaves (general), wood chips (trees)  
3) Apply: 5–10 cm layer, keep a gap around stems  
4) Maintain: top up as it decomposes

## 5. Mistakes to Avoid
- Too thin → little effect  
- Too thick → poor airflow  
- Touching stems → rot risk  
- Dry soil underneath → traps dryness

## 6. Biodynamic Insight
Mulch is a living interface between air and soil. Apply when the soil is receptive (slightly moist) and conditions are calm.
It’s not just a cover—it’s a rhythm keeper for moisture, temperature, and biology.

## 7. Results / Transformation
Cooler soil, reduced watering, fewer weeds, richer smell and texture.
Over time the garden becomes more stable and less reactive.

## 8. Quick Summary
- WHAT: cover soil with organic layer  
- WHEN: year-round (adapt thickness seasonally)  
- WHY: protect moisture, moderate temperature, feed soil life
