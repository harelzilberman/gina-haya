## What Is Compost Tea?

Compost tea is a biologically active liquid made by steeping mature compost in oxygenated water. The key word is **oxygenation** — without continuous airflow, beneficial aerobic bacteria can't multiply, and anaerobic organisms take over, potentially harming your plants.

The finished brew is rich in:

- **Beneficial bacteria** — build soil structure and break down organic matter
- **Mycorrhizal fungi** — partner with roots and dramatically increase nutrient uptake area
- **Protozoa** — "eat" bacteria and release plant-available nitrogen as waste
- **Humic acids** — improve soil aggregation and water retention

The difference between compost tea and plain compost is like the difference between a handful of dry soil and a cup of living liquid you can spray across 200 square meters in an hour. It spreads fast, reaches depths a hand can't touch, and colonises the plant from root to leaf — because foliar spraying carries beneficial microorganisms directly to the plant's defense sites.

## Step-by-Step Brewing Guide

### Step 1 — Dechlorinate Your Water

**Action:** Fill a 20–50 litre container with tap water and let it sit uncovered for 12–24 hours before brewing.

**Details:** Tap water chlorine kills microorganisms — the microbes cannot distinguish "good" from "bad." Passive aeration lets chlorine off-gas naturally. Rainwater works immediately with no waiting.

**Success signal:** The water smells clean, not chemical.

---

### Step 2 — Choose Mature Compost

**Action:** Select fully mature, finished compost — dark black, crumbly, earthy-smelling, with visible white fungal threads.

**Details:** Immature or "hot" compost still contains pathogens and anaerobic organisms that will transfer directly into the brew. Ratio: 1–2 kg per 20 litres of water.

**Success signal:** The compost smells like forest earth after rain — not sharp, not rotten.

### Step 3 — Prepare the Mesh Bag

**Action:** Place the compost inside a fine mesh bag, old nylon stocking, or muslin cloth. Tie it and suspend it inside the container.

**Details:** The bag allows free exchange of microorganisms between compost and water without releasing coarse particles that would clog your sprayer.

**Success signal:** The water begins turning golden-brown within hours.

---

### Step 4 — Add Microbial Food

**Action:** Add 1 teaspoon of unsulfured blackstrap molasses per 20 litres of water.

**Details:** The sugar acts as "fuel" for bacteria — it accelerates multiplication and increases active biomass. Don't overdo it. Too much sugar encourages yeast overgrowth that disrupts the microbial balance.

**Success signal:** The liquid becomes slightly cloudy after adding molasses.

---

### Step 5 — Aerate for 24–36 Hours

**Action:** Place an aquarium air pump (300–400 litres/hour for a 20L container) at the bottom. Run continuously for 24–36 hours.

**Details:** Aeration is the heart of the process. Bubbles oxygenate the water and allow aerobic bacteria to dominate. The stronger the aeration, the better the results.

**Success signal:** After 18–24 hours, the liquid smells like sweet forest earth — not sharp, not sour. Like a forest after autumn rain.

### Step 6 — Strain and Apply Immediately

**Action:** Remove the compost bag. Strain the liquid through cotton cloth into your application vessel.

**Details:** Use within 4 hours of finishing the brew. Every hour of delay reduces live organism count significantly. The leftover compost from the bag goes straight into the garden.

**Success signal:** The liquid is smooth, golden-brown, and pleasantly earthy.

## Common Mistakes

### Using tap water straight from the faucet

Chlorine kills microorganisms before they can multiply. You end up with brown water — not compost tea. **Fix:** Let water stand 12–24 hours, or use rainwater.

---

### Immature compost

Green or sharp-smelling compost contains pathogens and anaerobic organisms that transfer directly to the brew and then to your plants. **Fix:** Use only fully mature, black, crumbly compost.

---

### Weak aeration

Without enough oxygen, aerobic bacteria die within hours and anaerobes take over. **Fix:** Match pump capacity to container size — bubbles should rise throughout the entire container.

---

### Storing the brew

Without continuous aeration, organisms die within hours. What remains is an anaerobic liquid that can harm plants. **Fix:** Brew only what you need for one day. Use within 4 hours.

---

### Spraying in direct sun

UV light kills microorganisms within minutes. The liquid evaporates before it can be absorbed. **Fix:** Apply early morning (before 9am) or in the evening. Overcast days are ideal.

---

### Too much molasses

Creates yeast overgrowth that disrupts the desired microbial profile. **Fix:** One teaspoon per 20 litres — no more.

## Biodynamic Insight

In biodynamic gardening, compost tea is not just a tool — it is a **conversation with the soil**.

The biodynamic calendar distinguishes four types of days: Root, Leaf, Flower, and Fruit. For compost tea applied as a foliar spray or preventive treatment — **Leaf days** are optimal. On these days, the plant's forces concentrate in the foliage and green growth, maximising absorption through the leaves.

For soil rehabilitation — apply on a **Root day**. The root-zone irrigation aligns with the forces concentrated below the surface.

**Listen to the smell:** After a few weeks of regular application, the soil's scent will change. It will become deeper, richer — like a forest floor in autumn. This is not accidental. It's **geosmin** — a compound produced by *Streptomyces* bacteria, a biological signal of living soil.

Moderate soil moisture opens the pores for maximum uptake. Neither waterlogged nor bone-dry — the garden absorbs best at the midpoint.

## Results and Transformation

What happens after several weeks of regular use:

**In the plant:** Leaves develop a deep, dark, lustrous green — not the pale green of synthetic nitrogen. Stems thicken. Flowers and fruits become more abundant and heat-tolerant. Plants handle water stress better — leaves don't wilt by midday.

**In the soil:** Granular structure develops — the soil crumbles in your hand. Earthworm populations increase gradually. Water absorption improves — fewer puddles, more depth penetration.

**In the scent:** After two weeks — the soil smells richer, more complex. A faint but clear forest fragrance.

## Quick Summary

**What to do:**
1. Let tap water stand 12–24 hours (or use rainwater)
2. Mature compost in a mesh bag — 1–2 kg per 20 litres
3. One teaspoon molasses
4. Air pump running 24–36 hours
5. Strain and use **immediately** — early morning or evening

**When:**
- Foliar spray → Leaf day on the biodynamic calendar, before 9am
- Root watering → Root day, any quiet hour
- Frequency → Every two weeks through the growing season
- Best seasons → Spring and October–November (moderate temperature = happy bacteria)

**Why it works:**
Compost tea doesn't "feed" plants — it feeds the **soil** that feeds the plants. The microbial network established around the roots helps plants access nutrition they couldn't reach alone, withstand heat stress and drought, and repel pathogens from within.

> The healthiest soil is the one with the most life in it. Compost tea is a way of sowing life.
